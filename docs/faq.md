# Frequently Asked Questions

## How is a user study assigned to Level 1 or Level 2?

Assignment depends on the measured outcome: human judgments of factuality, clarity, usefulness, harmfulness, or another property of system output provide Level 1 evidence, whereas measures of comprehension, task completion, accessibility, or another user outcome in the evaluated task provide Level 2 evidence.

## How is deployment treated in the evidence levels?

Deployment establishes the setting in which evaluation occurs. Level 4 corresponds to direct measurement of an operational or service outcome such as processing time, workload, service uptake, referral completion, or institutional response. A deployed system evaluated through technical or output-quality measures is classified according to those measured outcomes.

## How is a randomized or field design treated?

Study design affects the credibility and scope of the estimate. Level 5 corresponds to measurement of the relevant social outcome for the defined population and context, so randomized, longitudinal, field, quasi-experimental, natural-experimental, and observational designs are classified according to the outcome they measure.

## How should the evidence levels be interpreted with respect to study quality?

The levels classify different objects of evaluation. Methodological quality is assessed within the demonstrated level, which allows a carefully conducted technical study and a carefully conducted field study to be appraised according to the questions each study addresses.

## Can a technical paper retain a broader social objective?

SOLIAS supports research programs in which a technical contribution is connected to a broader intended outcome while the current study reports the evidence it directly establishes. The intended outcome can therefore remain visible as a prospective objective alongside a bounded technical or output-level conclusion.

## Can a manuscript discuss an outcome that it has not directly evaluated?

Prospective outcomes may remain part of the manuscript when the wording identifies them as intended objectives. Expressions such as "may support," "is intended to support," or "is a component of" can describe that relationship while the reported result remains tied to the demonstrated outcome.

## What options are available when C is higher than E?

The paper identifies three responses: evaluate a missing transition, calibrate the conclusion to the demonstrated outcome, or identify the broader outcome as a subsequent evaluation target. The study's aims and available evidence determine which response is appropriate.

## Why report the complete `(E, C, G)` profile?

G alone omits the outcome layers being compared. For example, `(0, 0)` and `(4, 4)` both have a gap of zero, although they concern different objects of evaluation and therefore require different methodological appraisal.

## How are checklist items handled when they are not applicable?

The checklist permits an item to be designated not applicable when the reason is stated. Responses may be concise, and manuscript page references can be used where the relevant information is already reported.

## How should domain-specific guidance be organized?

Domain-specific guidance is maintained through profiles that preserve the six evidence levels and the `(E, C, G)` representation. A profile may add examples, measures, safeguards, or implementation guidance suited to its setting.

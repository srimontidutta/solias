# Evidence-Level Guide

The SOLIAS evidence level is the highest outcome layer directly evaluated for the relevant population and context. The classification identifies the measured outcome, while methodological appraisal addresses the credibility and scope of the evidence within that level.

## Level 0: Technical performance

**Direct evidence may include:** Accuracy, F1, calibration, robustness, retrieval quality, benchmark results, or performance on a stated technical task.

**Scope of supported conclusion:** A conclusion about system or method performance on the evaluated technical task, dataset, or test conditions.

## Level 1: Output quality

**Direct evidence may include:** Factuality, clarity, readability, harmfulness, usefulness, uncertainty expression, or expert and user judgments of outputs.

**Scope of supported conclusion:** A conclusion about the measured properties of the system's outputs under the stated evaluation conditions.

## Level 2: User outcome

**Direct evidence may include:** Comprehension, task completion, usability, time, confidence, error recovery, or accessibility in a controlled or realistic task.

**Scope of supported conclusion:** A conclusion about the measured user outcome for the evaluated population, task, and setting.

## Level 3: Decision or behavior

**Direct evidence may include:** Observed decisions or actions by users, practitioners, moderators, teachers, clinicians, caseworkers, or other institutional actors.

**Scope of supported conclusion:** A conclusion about the measured decision or behavioral outcome in the evaluated context.

## Level 4: Operational outcome

**Direct evidence may include:** Workflow performance, service uptake, referral completion, processing time, workload, institutional response, or deployment indicators.

**Scope of supported conclusion:** A conclusion about the measured operational or service outcome in the evaluated organization or setting.

## Level 5: Social outcome

**Direct evidence may include:** Health, learning, safety, inclusion, welfare, equity, environmental, or other population outcomes measured through an appropriate field design.

**Scope of supported conclusion:** A conclusion about the measured social outcome for the defined population and context.

## Assignment principles

Human participation can contribute to several evidence levels. A study in which participants judge output properties is classified at Level 1 when the output property is the measured object, while a study measuring comprehension or task success is classified at Level 2. Deployment provides an evaluation setting and reaches Level 4 when an operational outcome is measured. Experimental, quasi-experimental, longitudinal, natural-experimental, and observational designs can each be appropriate for particular questions; the evidence level records the outcome measured, and methodological appraisal addresses the credibility of the resulting inference.

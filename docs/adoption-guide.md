# Adoption Guide

SOLIAS can be used during study design, manuscript preparation, peer review, venue reporting, and evidence synthesis. The same outcome ontology is carried across these settings so that the demonstrated outcome and scope of the claim remain comparable.

## Study design

Begin with the intended outcome, target population, use context, and highest outcome the planned study will directly measure. The theory of change can then specify the intermediate user actions, institutional responses, assumptions, and dependencies connecting the NLP function to the intended outcome. These elements provide a basis for selecting measures that correspond to the outcome under evaluation.

The checklist can be opened during study design and updated as the work develops. Equity, accessibility, safety, failure pathways, and maintenance can therefore be considered alongside the planned outcome evaluation and incorporated into the study where they are relevant.

## Manuscript preparation

Assign E from the highest outcome directly evaluated and C from the highest outcome presented as a result. Report `(E, C, G)` together with the population, context, and measures supporting E. When `C > E`, the paper describes three available responses: evaluate a missing transition, calibrate the conclusion to the demonstrated outcome, or identify the broader outcome as a subsequent evaluation target.

The [author form](../templates/author-form.md) follows the complete 20-item checklist, and page references can be used in place of repeated prose when the manuscript already contains the relevant information.

## Peer review

Reviewers identify the demonstrated outcome, appraise methodological quality within that outcome layer, and then examine claim-evidence alignment. The [reviewer form](../templates/reviewer-form.md) reproduces the six-step sequence from the paper.

## Venue reporting

A venue can request the short fields described in the paper: `(E, C, G)`, the relevant population and context, the theory of change, and an evidence-calibrated conclusion. The [venue-adoption guide](venue-adoption.md) provides copy-ready wording and a compact form.

## Evidence synthesis

Systematic reviews and research syntheses can use E to group studies by the outcome directly demonstrated while C and G record how conclusions extend along the outcome pathway. The prospective checklist adds the population, mechanism, evaluation, responsible-operation conditions, and scope needed to interpret those records; the [evidence-synthesis guide](evidence-synthesis.md) describes the corresponding retrospective use.

## Domain profiles

Application areas can add examples, recommended measures, safeguards, and implementation guidance through profiles that preserve the six evidence levels and `(E, C, G)`, as described in the [domain-profile guidance](domain-profiles.md).

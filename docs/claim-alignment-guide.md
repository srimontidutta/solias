# Claim-Evidence Alignment Guide

SOLIAS applies the same six-level outcome ontology to demonstrated evidence and claimed outcomes. The profile `(E, C, G)` records the highest outcome directly evaluated, the highest outcome presented as a result, and the number of additional outcome transitions represented in the claim.

## Assign E

Identify the highest outcome layer directly evaluated for the relevant population and context. Measures and study materials should establish which outcome was observed; motivation, anticipated use, deployment setting, and study-design labels supply context for interpreting that evidence.

## Assign C

Identify the highest outcome presented as a result in the title, abstract, interpretation, discussion, or conclusion. Broader motivation and intended use can remain part of the paper when the wording presents them as prospective objectives.

## Calculate G

`G = max(0, C - E)`

When `G = 0`, the highest claimed outcome is at or below the demonstrated outcome. Positive values record the additional outcome transitions represented in the claim. E and C remain part of the reported profile so that equal values of G can be interpreted in relation to the outcome layers they connect.

## Interpret the profile

The profile is an ordinal description of claim-evidence alignment. Methodological appraisal proceeds within Level E and can address construct validity, uncertainty, population fit, comparison conditions, study design, safety, causal strength, and other considerations appropriate to the outcome.

## When C is higher than E

The paper describes three available responses: evaluation of a missing transition, calibration of the conclusion to the demonstrated outcome, or identification of the broader outcome as a subsequent evaluation target. Each response keeps the broader research objective connected to the study while locating the reported result at the outcome layer supported by the available evidence.

## Flexible claim template

> For [population] in [context], the system provides [NLP function]. In [evaluation], it changed [measured outcome] relative to [comparison], providing SOLIAS Level [E] evidence. The system is intended to contribute to [broader outcome], which requires evaluation of [next outcome] in [next setting].
